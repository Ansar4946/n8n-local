Why Automotive Rules and Tariff Demands Derailed the Deal

The auto sector became the primary dealbreaker when Washington introduced new tariff structures and stringent origin mandates for North American vehicle assembly. Canadian negotiators viewed these one-sided demands as crippling to domestic manufacturing. Without a stable framework for the auto industry, Ottawa refused to move forward.

Ontario Premier Doug Ford urged Ottawa not to accept a deal under pressure that would embolden further US demands. Ford's warning resonated strongly within provincial capitals that feared a slippery slope of endless concessions.

The auto industry in Ontario, Michigan, Kentucky, Indiana, and Ohio relies heavily on daily cross-border parts integration. As the talks collapsed, Canada promised to match US tariffs dollar for dollar to protect domestic vehicle and parts production.
